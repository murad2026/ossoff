The problem

Corruption isn't only bribes in envelopes. It's a system where corporate money buys access, lawmakers trade stocks in the industries they regulate, and the watchdogs who should catch abuse get sidelined. Since the Supreme Court's 2010 Citizens United decision, unlimited outside money has flooded elections, and voters see the result in what they pay for health care, housing and groceries.

“Corruption in America runs a lot deeper than Donald Trump.”

— Sen. Jon Ossoff, Savannah rally, June 2026 (reported by GPB)

What is Project 2025?

A roughly 900-page policy blueprint published by the Heritage Foundation, a conservative think tank, and written in part by people now in the administration, including White House budget director Russell Vought. During the 2024 campaign, Donald Trump distanced himself from it.

In office, the administration has adopted some of its central proposals: tightening White House control over agencies historically seen as independent, stripping civil-service protections from thousands of federal workers, and ending federal unions.

How much is already in place?

53% implementedby Heritage's own count, June 2026

A tracker run by the left-leaning Center for Progressive Reform reached a similar figure: 53% of the plan's domestic administrative agenda initiated or completed in the first year. Supporters of the plan say it delivers what voters elected; critics say it concentrates power and weakens checks on it. Read the trackers and decide for yourself.

The scorecard so far

What has gone into effect, what courts or Congress stopped, and the power play that backfired at the ballot box. All sourced below.

Put in place

  • Civil service. Protections stripped from thousands of federal workers; federal unions ended.
  • Independent agencies. Tighter White House control over agencies long seen as independent.
  • Foreign aid. USAID gutted, billions in aid halted.
  • Medicaid. Work requirements written into the 2025 budget law, due to start by the end of 2026.

Stopped or stalled

  • Education Department. Abolishing it needs an act of Congress, which hasn't passed. It has been cut back through the budget instead.
  • Consumer bureau. A federal judge blocked the shutdown of the CFPB.
  • Research funding. Caps on university research costs were blocked in court, and Congress rejected the proposed cuts.
  • Spending freezes. Courts blocked early attempts to halt spending Congress had already approved.

Backfired

  • Mid-decade redistricting. Not part of Project 2025, but a Trump-driven push in the same spirit. After a Supreme Court ruling weakened Voting Rights Act protection for majority-Black districts, Republican states redrew maps. AP's post-election review found the gamble fell flat in Alabama, Florida and Texas, was scuttled by courts in Missouri, and was offset by a Democratic redraw in California.
  • Black voters answered. Turnout ran at record or near-record midterm levels in several Southern states, and was especially strong where maps were redrawn. In Georgia the Black-white turnout gap hit a 20-year midterm low. About nine in ten Black voters backed Democratic House candidates.

Honest caveat: analysts note the returns don't by themselves prove why turnout rose. Anger over maps, the economy and decades of organizing all played a part.

How we'd fight it

Please read: this is a supporters' wish list, not Sen. Ossoff's platform. Each point builds on work he has already done, but he has not proposed this plan.

Make the stock-trading ban law

No individual stocks for members of Congress, the president, the vice president, cabinet officers or their spouses, with real penalties.

Builds on: his Ban Congressional Stock Trading Act

Get corporate money out

Ban corporate PACs, require disclosure of “dark money” donors, and pursue a constitutional amendment to overturn Citizens United.

Builds on: his corporate-PAC ban bill and his own no-corporate-PAC pledge

No profiting from the presidency

Presidents and vice presidents divest or use a true blind trust, release their tax returns, and can't take payments from foreign governments, with enforcement by law, not custom.

Builds on: his campaign theme that the presidency is not for sale

Protect the civil service

Write merit protections into law so career experts can't be reclassified and fired for political loyalty, whoever is president.

Builds on: his oversight work on how federal agencies are run

Independent watchdogs

Inspectors general and ethics officials who can only be removed for cause, with their reports made public.

Builds on: his investigations as chair of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations

Sunlight on lobbying

Real-time public disclosure of lobbying contacts and big donations, searchable by anyone.

Builds on: his A+ anti-corruption record and transparency push
