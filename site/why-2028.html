Our case

We can't afford to lose 2028.

This isn't an ordinary election cycle. Self-dealing at the top, allies left on their own, and a world that no longer trusts America's word all cost Americans real money and real security. If the facts aren't laid out plainly, people will stop believing that America can be fair or strong. So we're laying them out, with sources for every claim, and asking the Democrat with the cleanest record and the strongest win to run.

01 · At home

The presidency as a business

When foreign money can buy into the president's family business, every decision is open to doubt.

  • Foreign money in family crypto. Reuters found that foreign investors account for a significant share of the Trump family's crypto profits, and ethics experts called the arrangement an unprecedented conflict of interest.
  • A $500 million stake from a foreign royal. A senior member of the UAE's ruling family reportedly invested $500 million in the family-linked firm World Liberty Financial days before the inauguration, while the UAE was seeking access to advanced American AI chips.
  • Americans notice. More than six in ten Americans oppose the kind of conflicts of interest that have marked this term, according to a series of 2026 polls.

The White House says the president's assets are in a trust run by his children and that there are no conflicts of interest. Mr. Trump said he was unaware of the UAE investment.

02 · Security

Our technology edge, for a fee

America's lead in AI chips is a security asset. It shouldn't be traded for a cut of the sales.

  • Advanced chips cleared for China. In December 2025 the president approved sales of Nvidia's H200 AI chips to approved customers in China, with the government taking a 25% cut, despite warnings that the chips could strengthen China's military.
  • Both parties objected. Democratic senators warned the chips could aid China's military and cyberattacks, and Republican members of the House China committee raised national-security concerns of their own.
  • Even the administration is split. In November 2026, after a clash between the Treasury and Commerce secretaries, Commerce tightened rules to stop chips leaking to China through third countries.

The administration says the most advanced chips are reserved for U.S. companies and that sales come with security conditions, including a ban on military use.

03 · Allies

Ukraine and Europe, left on their own

Walking away from a democracy under attack tells every ally, and every adversary, what America's word is worth.

  • New aid stopped. The administration halted approval of new military aid packages for Ukraine when it took office in January 2025.
  • Weapons and intelligence paused. After the February 2025 Oval Office clash with President Zelensky, all U.S. military aid, including intelligence sharing, was suspended for more than a week.
  • Money Congress approved, held back. In July 2026 the Pentagon told Congress it would not finish spending $400 million already approved for Ukraine until fiscal year 2029, the year after this president leaves office. Members of both parties objected.
  • Celebrated, not regretted. Vice President Vance called the end of Ukraine aid one of the administration's proudest achievements.

The administration says it favors a negotiated settlement and that Europe should carry more of the cost of its own defense.

05 · Your wallet

Why it hits the economy

This is our argument, not a forecast. It's why we think the trust collapse is a kitchen-table issue.

  1. Allies stop counting on us. Partners who doubt U.S. commitments hedge by buying weapons, chips and energy elsewhere and making new trade deals without us.
  2. Investors demand more. Money flows to places with predictable rules. When policy looks for sale, investors either stay away or charge a premium, and that shows up in borrowing costs.
  3. Exports and jobs follow. Fewer buyers abroad means fewer orders for American factories and farms.
  4. Division at home deepens. When people believe the system is rigged, they stop trusting institutions, elections and each other, and a divided country can't fix any of this.
